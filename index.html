Despite decades of research on autism, we still know little about how social behavior breaks down, and even less about how it normally works. My goal is to enhance our understanding of the basic principles of social behavior, including why it differs between males and females, and to use this knowledge to contribute to research on the causes and treatment of disorders with social deficits.

During my Ph.D. at the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), I studied the synaptic and circuit mechanisms behind social deficits in Shank2-mutant mice, a model of autism. I focused on parvalbumin (PV) interneurons and on how their excitatory synapses and electrical synapses, formed by neuronal gap junctions, work together.

Now at the Salk Institute, I study aromatase-expressing neurons, the cells that make estrogen in the brain. I examine how neuroestrogen levels change during social interaction and how these circuits shape sex differences in social behavior and brain development. I combine mouse genetics with fiber photometry, viral circuit tools, and spatial transcriptomics.

Research highlights

Social cognition needs both chemical and electrical synapses in PV interneurons

In Shank2-mutant mice, a model of autism, I asked which cells and synaptic mechanisms drive the behavioral changes. Deleting Shank2 only in parvalbumin (PV) interneurons caused hyperactivity and enhanced self-grooming, while social interaction stayed normal and seizure susceptibility decreased. In the cortex, NMDA receptors and neuronal gap junctions in PV interneurons work together to produce the high-frequency burst firing that supports social cognition. This cooperation is weakened in Shank2-mutant mice, and boosting PV neuron bursts with optogenetics restored cortical social cognition.

Lee S*, Lee E*, et al., Frontiers in Molecular Neuroscience 2018
Lee E*, Lee S*, et al., Nature Communications 2021

Gene dosage reshapes autism-related phenotypes and their sex differences

The effects of an autism-linked gene can depend on how many copies are lost and on the age and sex of the animal. In the prefrontal cortex of Shank2-mutant mice, losing one copy or both copies shifted gene expression in opposite directions in juveniles, while adults of both genotypes showed autism-like changes. In Chd8-mutant mice, a homozygous mutation produced stronger autism-related phenotypes than a heterozygous one and reduced the sex differences seen in heterozygous mice.

Kim J*, Lee S*, Hwang E*, et al., Molecular Psychiatry 2026
Lee S*, et al., Frontiers in Molecular Neuroscience 2021

Software and code

Web app
Allen CCF Explorer

A browser-based 3D viewer of the Allen Mouse Brain Common Coordinate Framework, built for planning stereotaxic surgery. Targets are entered in mediolateral, anteroposterior, and dorsoventral coordinates and checked on coronal, sagittal, and horizontal sections. Probe angles can be set to match a specific stereotaxic manipulator. Path analysis lists every brain region an implant passes through, with entry and exit depths, and exports the result as SVG or CSV. It runs from a single HTML file without installation.

Analysis code
Cilia stroke analysis

MATLAB code that measures ciliary beat frequency of ependymal cells lining the lateral ventricle from high-speed video recorded at about 1,000 frames per second. Regions of interest are placed along user-drawn lines, pixel intensity changes over time are extracted from each region, and a fast Fourier transform gives a representative beat frequency for each region. Used in Kang et al., PLoS Biology 2024.
